# H-interleave · team-draft interleaving as a faster comparison

- **Status:** built, not deployed (14 Nov)
- **Owner:** main session
- **Claim:** showing one list that mixes two strategies, and crediting each click or purchase to the strategy that contributed the product, tells which strategy picks the products shoppers want with fewer requests than a traffic split, because every request compares both sides on the same shopper at the same moment.
- **Test:** `duel1` (A = ranker2, B = blend1, module constants). Per round a coin from sha1(request_id) decides who picks first; each side adds its best product not yet shown and is credited with it. 5 products per side when both lists have them. Credit goes to `team_01.scratch` as `kind = 'duel'` rows. `uv run scripts/duel.py [minutes]` gives, per pair and per hour, the B share of credited clicks and of credited purchases, a two-sided sign test and a Wilson 95% interval. Only requests we served whose shown list holds every credited product count.
- **What it measures:** when both strategies' products are on the same screen, whose products get clicked and bought. It is a preference test.
- **What it does not:** the conversion of either full list. Each side shows only 5 products, in slots the coin assigns, next to the other side's products. A strategy can win the duel and still lose as a full list, for example when its strength is the order of the whole top 10 or the complementarity of its products. A duel win filters; promotion still needs a split (PLAN section 7). Products both lists rank go to the side that picked them, so heavy overlap dilutes the signal toward 0.5.
- **Rough power (sign test, alpha 0.05 two-sided, power 80%):** credited events needed for a true B share of 0.524 / 0.55 / 0.60 are about 3,400 / 780 / 190. At 2.0% purchases per request that is about 170k / 39k / 9.7k duel requests; at 6% clicks about 57k / 13k / 3.2k. All traffic is 10,800 requests per hour, so a duel arm at 25% (2,700 per hour) settles a 0.60 purchase share in about 4 h and a 0.55 click share in about 5 h. If B's products simply convert 10% better, the share is about 1.1 / 2.1 = 0.524, and the duel needs about as many requests as a split (2.0% vs 2.2% needs about 80k requests per arm). The gain comes from clicks (three times as many events) and from preferences stronger than the full-list lift, which is what interleaving usually finds; this is what the first run should tell us.
