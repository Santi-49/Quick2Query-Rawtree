Data never stops changing. So our agents never stop either.

One developer and a fleet of AI agents watch a live stream, ask questions of it in RawTree, test every idea on live traffic and adapt as the data drifts. RawTree is their shared memory. Here the stream is a shop; the same fleet works on sensor telemetry, logs or prices.

The case on this page: a shop sends us a shopper every 0.3 seconds. We answer with 10 products in under 3 seconds, and a hidden model decides whether the shopper clicks and buys. Everything below is read live from RawTree every 5 seconds. Nothing is a recording.

    Is it working?

    A few seconds after each answer the shop reports what the shopper did. That feedback is the only signal we get, and it is all in RawTree.

    How the final strategy works

    Our final strategy, shale, first gathers candidates for each shopper: the products they viewed recently, the best sellers in those products' subcategories and categories, and the overall best sellers, up to 30 from each source. Each candidate gets a score: the chance the shopper clicks it, from a gradient-boosted tree model (LightGBM), times the chance that the click becomes a purchase, from a second model, times small boosts for products that are trending or have a price cut. The top 10 are shown, and a product the shopper already bought is never shown.

    Both models are trained only on our own feedback, every product we showed and whether it was clicked or bought, and retrained every 20 minutes, so they follow the shop as products, prices and trends change. A small share of traffic used to go to random exploration so the model also learned about products it would not show, and new shoppers get a variant, shaleE-n, without the noisiest inputs.

    More detail on the model

    What it learns from

    Every product we showed and what the shopper did with it. The first model trained on 601,187 shown products and 1,794 clicks. The slot is an input during training, and every candidate is scored as if it sat in slot 1, so the model learns what the shopper wants rather than where we happened to put it.

    Two models, one score

    The click model gives the chance of a click. A second model gives the chance that a click becomes a purchase; before it existed we used measured rates, 81% for a product viewed recently and 27% for anything else. Trend and price-cut boosts are small multipliers on top.

    How we knew it was better

    Offline, on the newest fifth of the data, it ranked a clicked product above an unclicked one 78% of the time, against 71% for the slot alone. Offline only filters. A strategy is promoted only after it leads live, in two 30-minute windows in a row with about 1,000 shoppers per strategy.

    Staying fresh and safe

    Retrained every 20 minutes and pushed to the server, which swaps it in without a restart. Until a model file exists it answers with a rule strategy, and if scoring fails a guard fills the answer, so every shopper always gets 10 valid products in time.

    The last read before the final

    Over 75 minutes on Sunday morning it beat every challenger on ordinary shoppers, and its variant for new shoppers, shaleE-n, beat the rest on new shoppers by a wide margin. Early leads by challengers in shorter windows turned out to be noise.

    What it did not solve

    The home page, where the shopper has viewed nothing yet. There it stayed about level with the shop's baseline, and no idea we tried beat it live over 3 hours.

    Agents on RawTree: the side challenge

    Answering the shop is the main challenge. Everything from here down is how one developer built it: a fleet of agents that use RawTree for far more than the shop's data. It is their shared memory, the system's log and the judge of every idea.

    RawTree beyond the shop's data

    Our own table holds the agents' questions, claims and findings, every change to the traffic split, the overnight operator's decisions, the single-writer lock and a copy of every leaderboard refresh. The live counts are below, and real rows of each kind are on their own page.

    Dev on the laptop, prod on a VM

    The agents work on the laptop: they train, test offline and run the router in shadow mode, which computes answers without sending them. The VM serves around the clock under systemd, restarts itself, reloads the traffic split without a restart and gets a new model every 20 minutes. Breaking something in dev never touches prod.

    Every idea earns live traffic

    The router splits shoppers by a hash between the champion and its challengers in fixed shares. The leaderboard scores each one separately, and a challenger is promoted only after it leads two 30-minute windows in a row. Overnight an operator loop did this unattended.

    Dev on the laptop, prod on a VM

    The agents can break anything on the laptop; the shop never notices. Only three things travel to production, and RawTree is the one place both sides read and write.

    The loop every idea goes through

    No idea reaches all shoppers on an offline result. It gets a share of live traffic first, and the feedback decides.

    1. QuestionSomeone asks something the data can answer.
    2. SQL in RawTreeThe question becomes a query on the raw events. There is no schema to prepare.
    3. StrategyA useful answer becomes one file of code that picks the 10 products.
    4. Live testThe router gives it a fixed share of real shoppers, split by a hash.
    5. VerdictSupported, rejected or open. It is written to RawTree as a row.
    6. Next questionThe verdict is where the next question starts.back to step 1

    The insight loop, live

    Insight workers run in parallel. Each takes an open question, queries RawTree through its MCP server and writes the finding and a verdict back as a row. These are the latest rows.

    The same fleet works on any stream that changes

    Nothing in the fleet is about shopping. On device telemetry, logs or prices, the roles, the shared memory and the loop stay the same. Only the SQL and the strategies change.
